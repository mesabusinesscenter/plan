The market, and what we should charge

Class B office$21.82What a generic suite gets

The pricing decision. Medical office carries a 38 percent premium over general office in this market. We have a medical building by county record, a licensed clinic as the anchor, C-G zoning and nearly three acres of parking. We should market professional and wellness suites in the high twenties, not compete for general office tenants at $22. The clinic is the amenity, and nobody else on this stretch has one.

What we are competing against

  • 29 available spaces in the Superstition Corridor at roughly $25 — mostly plain office with no anchor and no draw.
  • Newer Class A product that beats us on finish and loses to us on rate and parking.
  • The Palo District eventually — newer and more expensive when it delivers, which is exactly why our window is now.

What none of them have: a health system as landlord, referral flow from an operating clinic, free parking on three acres, an Opportunity Zone designation, and a mission that lets us say yes to a tenant a commercial landlord would not underwrite.

04

Three anchors first

Anchors set the rent and bring the traffic. Everything after them leases faster and for more. With fifteen suites open, this is the moment to be deliberate rather than take the first caller.

1 · Clinical

Optometry, physical therapy, podiatry, behavioral health, lab or imaging. Pays the medical-office rate and feeds on referral flow from day one.

2 · Food

A small coffee shop or café. The patio is already built. It gives people a reason to come when they do not have an appointment.

3 · Service

A credit union or CDFI branch, legal aid, or tax preparation. The 24,518 people within a mile drive somewhere else for these today.

Where the clinical names come from

Our own referral data. Which outside providers are we sending patients to, and how often. That report already exists inside our system and it is the target list. They serve our people and they already drive here.

Why the café is not a small idea

It is the cheapest thing we can do to change what the property feels like. Shaded tables already sit under the arcade facing the planted court. We are offering a patio that exists, not asking a tenant to build one. Price it as a draw, not as a rent line.

The offer

We will not win on rate and should not try. We win on terms, on what comes with the space, and on who the landlord is.

ElementWhat we offer
Months 1 and 2Half rent, on a term of 36 months or longer
Month 3 onwardFull contract rent
ImprovementsA published allowance per square foot, the same for everyone, not negotiated deal by deal
SignageOn the monument and the building, included
ParkingFree, nearly three acres, much of it shaded by solar canopy
Business supportMesa Business Builder coaching and a sponsored ASBA membership, arranged by us
ReferralsClinical tenants are introduced to our care teams
DecisionIn writing, within five business days of the tour

Why two months at half rent is the right number. On a 1,200 square foot suite at $26, the concession is about $2,600. One month of vacancy on that suite costs the entire operating expense with no revenue against it — it still gets cooled, insured, secured, lit and maintained. The concession costs less than the vacancy it prevents, and it buys a three-year term.

These terms are a working draft and have not been approved.

07

Incentives — what is real and who can use it

The Opportunity Zone

Our tract, 4222.03, is a designated Opportunity Zone, confirmed in IRS Notice 2018-48. An investor who puts a capital gain into a qualified fund here defers the tax and, after ten years, pays nothing on the appreciation.

NATIVE HEALTH cannot use this. We are a 501(c)(3) with no capital gains to defer, so it is worth nothing to us directly. It is worth a great deal to the people we are recruiting — a tenant financing a build-out, a developer looking at excess land, a donor holding appreciated stock. That is a recruiting argument and we should be making it in writing.

The current designation runs through December 31, 2028. The 2025 federal tax act made the program permanent, and governors began nominating the next ten-year map on July 1, 2026, with new designations effective January 1, 2027. Whether our tract is re-designated is an open question and a direct ask for the City of Mesa.

08

How we organize ourselves

This property is the test of whether we can run real estate professionally. We hold eleven shopping centers’ worth of institutional memory from the Navajo Nation years and almost none of the current discipline. This is the part that outlasts the leasing push.

Who owns what

FunctionOwnerStandard
LeasingOne named person, not a committeePipeline reported monthly; a prospect gets a decision within five business days
Rent rollFinanceCurrent to the month; no tenant past 60 days without a written plan
Lease approvalApproval matrix — staff below a threshold, CEO aboveWritten, so nobody has to ask who signs
Lease formCounsel, with ComplianceOne standard form; deviations logged and approved, never improvised
MaintenanceFacilitiesLogged, ticketed, closed; response time tracked
Property managementOrenda, under a scopeDeliverables and dates in the scope, reviewed quarterly

The separation that has to hold. The person who signs a lease should not be the person who collects the rent, and neither should approve the improvement spend. That is ordinary internal control, it is what Compliance will ask about, and it is far easier to put in place now than to retrofit after the building fills.

The systems we need

  • A rent roll that is one document — suite, size, tenant, rate, term, expiry, escalations, options, deposit. Today this is scattered.
  • Lease abstracts — one page per lease, so nobody reads forty pages to answer a question.
  • A leasing pipeline — every prospect, stage, next action and date. A spreadsheet is enough; the discipline matters more than the software.
  • A work order system with a tenant-facing way to report a problem and a record of when it closed.
  • A tenant file per tenant — lease, insurance certificate, business license, contacts, correspondence.
  • A published rate card and standard lease. Negotiating each deal from scratch is how small portfolios lose money.

The rhythm

CadenceWhat happens
WeeklyLeasing pipeline reviewed — every prospect gets a next action and a date
MonthlyRent roll, aging, occupancy and work orders to Finance and the Executive Team
QuarterlyOccupancy against the 90 percent standard, renewals in the next twelve months, capital needs, vendor review
AnnuallyRate card reset against market comps; reserve funded against the capital plan

The numbers we hold ourselves to

  • 90 percent occupancy, stated as a standard and reported alongside the clinical numbers.
  • Five business days from tour to written decision.
  • Zero tenants past 60 days in arrears without a written payment plan.
  • Twelve months’ notice before any lease expiry — renewals start a year out, not a month out.
  • A funded reserve. The cooling situation is what an unfunded reserve looks like, and H&R Block going dark is what it costs in rent.

The basis, stated plainly. Suite sizes have not been measured yet. The October 30 inventory replaces every square-foot figure below. What follows is a model built on a stated mix, not a forecast, and it is here so the inventory lands on a frame rather than a blank page.

The margin is the real argument. We are already paying to run this building. The water, the power, the cooling plant, the insurance, the security, the landscaping and the management are being paid on the whole property today, whether a suite is occupied or empty. Filling a suite does not add a building — it adds a tenant to one we already carry. Most of that rent reaches the bottom line, which is not true of a development project and is the reason Mesa is the fastest revenue in the system.

One honest caveat on cooling. The plant is already sized and already being paid for, so occupancy adds marginal load rather than a new system. But it does add load. The mini-split decision in December should be sized for a full building, not for the building as it sits today. Deciding it against current occupancy would mean solving this twice.

For comparison, the Seed incubator model at 16th Street totals $103,600 a year across suites, coworking, the sewing lab, technical services and events. Mesa stabilized is roughly 2.3 times that, from space that already exists.

That is not a coincidence we should watch happen. It is the single best reason a clinical tenant should take space here rather than a mile east.

Those are names, not rumors, and they are the people to be in a room with before the district opens rather than after.

Where the collaborations are

Five of these are available to us now, cost little, and none of them require the building to be full first.

Banner Desert

1,052 physicians across 75 specialties, a mile and a half away. Some of them already see our patients. A specialist who wants a second day a week on this side of Dobson is our clinical anchor, and the referral relationship is the introduction.

Mesa Community College

Allied health, nursing and dental hygiene students need clinical placements. We need workforce. Their small business programs and our incubator want the same people. And a campus of that size a mile west is a café’s customer base.

Blue Cross Blue Shield of Arizona

Already committed to a wellness campus three quarters of a mile from us. A health plan building here has reasons to care what a community health center on the same street is doing.

The member nations

Eighteen have citizens in Maricopa County. A nation has leased here before to serve its members. That suite is open again, and the conversation is one we have already proved.

City of Mesa

Economic Development can put us in a prospectus, answer the Opportunity Zone question, and connect tenants to Business Builder coaching. We have not asked yet.

WaveX and the venture studio

An AI venture studio backed by LG is landing on this corridor. Our incubator is the nearest thing to it on Southern Avenue. Worth a conversation before someone else has it.

The three that would move the most

  1. A standing referral conversation with Banner Desert. It serves patients, it names clinical tenants, and it costs a meeting.
  2. A clinical placement agreement with Mesa Community College. Students rotate through our clinic, we see the workforce before we hire it, and the college has reason to point its people at this address.
  3. A seat at the table with the Palo District before it opens. A $2.5 billion development three quarters of a mile away will need community health partners, Native vendors and local hiring. We should be known to them in 2026, not in 2028.
